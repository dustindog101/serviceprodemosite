## 2. What the leaders do poorly (our opening)
- Franchise sites feel generic/corporate, dense mega-menus, A/B clutter. Local sites have typos, hidden testimonials, no city pages, no FAQs, no schema evidence.
- Rainbow hides the CTA in hero; Paul Davis buries city names. A single-operator local brand with real faces, named reviews, hard response promises, and clean local pages can beat them.

## 3. Recommended page blueprint (home)
1. Slim top bar: "24/7 Emergency - Serving DC, MD & NoVA" + phone (tel:).
2. Header: logo, 5 nav items max, persistent "Call Now" button (mobile: sticky bottom bar with Call + Get Help Now).
3. Hero: H1 "24/7 Water, Fire & Mold Restoration in [DC / Maryland / Northern Virginia]"; one-line promise ("On-site in 60 minutes or less" - only if true); primary button = big phone number, secondary = short form/text-us; rating chip (e.g., 4.9 / N Google reviews); 3 badges (IICRC Certified, Licensed & Insured, Direct Insurance Billing). Real crew/van photo, not stock.
4. Trust strip: stats (response time, years, jobs completed, carriers worked with) + insurer logos if permitted.
5. "What happened?" service cards: Water, Fire & Smoke, Mold, Sewage/Storm, Reconstruction - each linked.
6. Process: Call > Inspect & Extract > Dry & Document > Restore (4 steps, with timing).
7. Insurance section: "We work with your adjuster, we document everything, direct billing."
8. Reviews (named, with neighborhood, Google source) + before/after gallery.
9. Service areas: linked city/county grid (Arlington, Alexandria, Fairfax, Bethesda, Silver Spring, Rockville, DC wards...).
10. FAQ (cost, drying time, insurance, what to do before we arrive) with FAQPage schema.
11. Final CTA + short form ("Is this an emergency?" toggle, name, phone, ZIP, type of damage; 4 fields max) + footer with NAP.

## 4. Service page structure (copy SERVPRO's proven order)
H1 (service + geo) > 3 value bullets + call button > badges > intro > sub-service cards > "What to do right now" (3 tips) > why us > process > insurance > before/after case study > FAQ (8-10) > related services > closing CTA. Include a local line per page and links to 2-3 nearby service-area pages.

## 5. Local SEO
- Service-area pages per city/county (/water-damage-restoration/arlington-va/): unique H1 "Water Damage Restoration in Arlington, VA", local housing/risk context (basements, rowhouses, older plumbing, flash flooding), local reviews, nearby-area links, embedded map. Avoid thin duplicated templates.
- URL pattern: /services/{service}/ and /service-areas/{city-state}/ consistently (1-800 mixes paths - avoid).
- H1 patterns: "24/7 {Service} in {City}, {State}"; title tag "{Service} {City} | 24/7 Emergency | {Brand}" under 60 chars; meta with phone + response promise.
- Keep NAP identical to Google Business Profile; link GBP, embed reviews; Maps link in footer.
- Schema (JSON-LD): LocalBusiness subtype (HomeAndConstructionBusiness / GeneralContractor), openingHours 24/7 (Mo-Su 00:00-23:59), areaServed (City list), telephone, priceRange, aggregateRating (only real, visible reviews), sameAs, hasOfferCatalog; Service per service page; FAQPage; BreadcrumbList.

## 6. Premium feel / conversion tips
- Calm authority over panic: deep navy or charcoal with one strong emergency accent (orange/red for call button); white space; large type. Avoid stock-photo clutter. (Approx competitor palettes: SERVPRO green/orange, PuroClean blue/red, Paul Davis blue/red, Rainbow multi-color.)
- Type: one geometric/grotesque sans for headings (Inter / Manrope / Plus Jakarta Sans), 16-18px body, system or same family for body; fine-weight numerals for stats.
- Real photos: crew, uniformed techs, branded vehicle, equipment (dehumidifiers/air movers), moisture meter, before/after.
- Specific, verifiable promises beat adjectives ("Call answered by a person in under 30 seconds", "Tech on site within 60 min").
- Humanize: team page, tech names/photos, "who will arrive" reassurance.
- Subtle motion only (fade-in, count-up stats); no heavy sliders/carousels (PuroClean's carousel repeats reviews).
- Make it feel local: neighborhood names, local landmarks in imagery.

## 7. Emergency-site best practices
**Click-to-call:** every phone number is `tel:` link; sticky mobile bottom bar (Call + Text/Form) and header button; track call clicks as conversions (GA4 event + call tracking with dynamic number insertion that keeps the static NAP in schema/footer).
**Forms:** 3-4 fields (name, phone, ZIP/address, damage type), phone input `type=tel` with autocomplete attributes, "Is this an emergency?" toggle that promotes call-now messaging, large tap targets (min 44-48px), inline validation, confirmation promising a callback time, spam protection that is invisible (honeypot/Turnstile), form posts to email/SMS alert immediately.
**Core Web Vitals (target mobile, 75th percentile):** LCP < 2.5s (compress hero to AVIF/WebP, preload hero image, no lazy-load on it, fetchpriority=high), INP < 200ms (minimal JS, defer third-party chat/tracking), CLS < 0.1 (set image dimensions, reserve space for sticky bar and badges). Self-host/subset fonts (font-display: swap), critical CSS inline, static HTML if possible, CDN.
**Accessibility/trust:** WCAG AA contrast (esp. orange on white buttons), visible focus states, alt text, HTTPS, privacy/terms links, license numbers and insurance in footer.
**Content hygiene:** proof-read city names, one consistent address, no placeholder popups (issues seen on competitors).
